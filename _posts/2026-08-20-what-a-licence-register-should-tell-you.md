---
title: What a mining licence register should tell you
date: 2026-08-20
description: Most public licence registers answer the wrong question. Here's the shorter list that actually matters.
tags: [extractives, open data]
---

Most public mining licence registers publish what the ministry happens to keep
in its filing system: a licence number, a holder name, a polygon, and a status
field with four possible values. That is a record of administration, not a
record of a decision.

The question a citizen actually has is narrower. Who got the right to extract
something publicly owned, when, on what terms, and who stands behind the
company that holds it.

Four fields carry most of that weight.

**Award date, separately from the validity period.** A licence valid from 2019
to 2044 tells you nothing about when the decision was made. If the register
collapses the two, you cannot ask whether awards cluster in the months before
an election — which is, in several countries, exactly the question worth asking.

**The holder's incorporation date.** A company constituted six weeks before it
received a licence is a different object from one that has been operating for
twenty years. This is a cheap field to publish and a very expensive one to
reconstruct afterwards.

**Beneficial ownership, as structured data.** Not a PDF of a declaration form.
Names, dates of birth to the year, ownership percentages, in columns.

**Whatever preceded the award.** An auction, a first-come application, a
direct negotiation, a conversion from an older permit. Without it, every
licence looks equally routine.

None of this requires a new system. It requires deciding that the register is a
public accountability instrument rather than an internal ledger that happens to
be visible.
